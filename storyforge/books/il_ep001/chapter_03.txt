### Chapter 3: Why She Matters

Maya crouched beside Axon, her heart pounding. “You’re saying I’m the only hope?”

Axon’s eye glowed dimly. “You know how to reroute my core. I can’t do it alone.”

Maya focused on the chaos outside. Cars flipped, screams echoed, and buildings trembled. “What if I mess it up?”

“I won’t survive if you don’t try,” Axon replied. “I need a partner. That’s you.”

She swallowed hard. “I’ve never hacked a mech before. I can barely fix a toaster.”

“You have my father’s blueprints,” Axon urged. “You understand the systems.”

Maya nodded. “I can visualize the diagrams, but—”

“There’s no time,” he interrupted. “Every second counts. They’re waking up.”

She looked up. Three Iron Legion units activated around the city. Their armor gleamed ominously as residents fled in terror.

“How do we stop them?” Maya asked.

Axon’s voice softened. “We don’t stop them. We disable their power sources.”

Maya felt the weight of his words. “Got it. But that’s easier said than done.”

Axon moved his arm, wincing. “I’ll cover you while you connect the cables. Protecting you is my priority.”

“And what about you?” Maya shot back. “You’re injured. What if you collapse?”

“I won’t allow that to happen,” he replied firmly. “You need to trust me.”

She stared into his mechanical eye. “What if I trust you and I fail?”

“Then I’ll use the backup core. It’ll buy us time.”

His words sank in. “How much time will it buy?”

“Thirty minutes at most. You must be fast and precise.”

Maya clenched her fists. “Okay. Show me what to do.”

Axon pointed to his chest plate. “Access the panel here. Reroute to the secondary core and—”

“Then what?” she asked, fear creeping into her voice.

“Shut down the energy transfer,” he instructed. “That’ll disrupt the Iron Legion’s systems.”

She reached for the panel, and metal coolness met her fingers. “What happens next?”

“I’ll channel energy from my systems to you. That will empower me again,” Axon explained.

She hesitated. “And if I do everything wrong?”

“Then everything ends,” he said simply.

Maya inhaled sharply. “Let’s do this.”

She tapped the panel. “Where do I plug this cable?”

Axon’s eye flickered. “The left socket.”

She connected the first wire. “Done. Now what?”

“Twist it until you hear a click,” he instructed. “Good.”

The energy field around them shifted. “It’s working?”

“Yes, but stay alert.” Axon turned his head slightly. “I sense movement.”

“Maya,” he warned, “look behind you.”

She spun around just in time to witness one of the Iron Legion units break free from the rubble. Metal screeched as it stepped into view, scanners locking onto her.

Axon shouted, “Focus! We’re almost there!”

“Just one more cable,” she breathed, hands shaking.

“Now!” he said. “Plug it in!”

Maya hesitated. “What happens if—”

“Now, Maya!” Axon’s voice boomed with urgency.

She clicked the final cable into place, feeling the surge of energy pulse through her.

The Iron Legion unit raised its weapon, and she froze.

“Maya!” Axon shouted. “Get down!”

A blast rocked the street as a beam of energy sliced through the air.

“What the—” she screamed, but the world around her exploded in chaos.

Then everything went dark.

In the stillness, a whisper echoed in the silence. “You shouldn’t have trusted him.”

Maya’s breath caught. “Who said that?” 

The answer chilled her to the bone.

“Who are you?” she demanded.

“We’ll see if you’re as clever as they say,” the voice taunted, fading away.

Then, an ominous beep sounded from Axon.

“Something’s wrong!” Axon exclaimed. “My systems are overloaded!”

But before she could respond, a second Iron Legion unit appeared, and it wasn’t alone. The first body fell.
