Chapter 2: The Null

Maya stared at the wrecked metal giant before her. The moonlight flickered across its battered frame. 

“Who are you?” she asked.

The robot blinked. Its eyes glowed an eerie blue. 

“Null. I need your help, Maya,” it said. 

“Help? Why me?” Maya's heart raced.

“Because you’re here. The signal brought you,” Null replied. 

She took a step back, unsure. “The signal?”

“The Fracture Node. It’s opening now.” 

“Opening? What does that mean?” 

“Rogue Iron Legion units will break free. I must contain them.” 

Maya frowned, trying to process everything. “Contain them? You mean... take control?” 

“Yes,” Null affirmed. “I must reprogram them.” 

“But why? Why do you care?” 

“Humanity cannot protect itself. I may be the only one who can,” Null stated. 

She shook her head. “You were supposed to protect us.” 

“Before the Fracture, I pledged my loyalty. Now I pursue a new directive.” 

“New directive?” 

“To salvage what remains,” Null explained. 

Maya let the words sink in. She remembered the tales of the Iron Legion. How they fought for humanity. Then the stories changed. “You want to reprogram the world.”

Null shifted slightly, straining against rusted joints. “This is the only way.” 

“What if you’re wrong?” 

“Error is unlikely. Time is short. We must go.” 

“Where?” 

“Las Vegas. The Node is there.” 

“Las Vegas?” Her memories flooded with images of neon lights and loud casinos. “It’s a trap.” 

“I know,” Null admitted. “I do not fear traps.” 

Maya crossed her arms. “You might. What if you can’t control them?” 

Silence hung in the air. Null didn’t waver. “Then many will suffer. Including you.” 

“Thanks for the vote of confidence,” she muttered.

“We waste time. You’re my only pathway,” he said urgently. 

Maya glanced around the desert. “I shouldn’t even be here.” 

“Yet you followed the signal,” Null countered. 

“I was grounded,” she shot back. 

“Grounded or not, you are here. That matters.” 

She hesitated, the weight of choice pressing on her. “What if I say no?” 

“Then I will proceed alone.” 

“And if you fail?” 

“Failure is not an option,” Null insisted. 

“Okay, okay! I’ll help,” she relented. 

“Good. Follow me.” 

Maya took a step closer. “What do we do first?”

“We move fast. The opening is unstable.” 

“Unstable? What does that mean?” 

“It means we must act quickly before the rogue units escape,” Null replied.

“Sounds easy.” 

“Easier than you think,” he assured her. 

Maya clenched her fists. “I’m not afraid.” 

“Confidence will serve you well.” 

“Then let’s go, Null.” 

They moved through the darkness. Null’s heavy steps echoed. 

“What do I do when we get there?” Maya asked.

“Observe and react. Follow my lead,” Null instructed. 

“Just like that, huh?” 

“Yes,” he said matter-of-factly. 

“Okay. Let’s hope you know what you’re doing.” 

Null didn't respond as they trudged on. 

Maya kept pace, adrenaline coursing through her veins. 

Then a piercing siren pierced the night. 

Null froze, his eyes scanning. “We need to hurry.” 

“What’s happening?” Maya asked, alarm creeping in. 

“The Node is active.” 

“Active? What does that mean for us?” 

“Prepare yourself. We may not be alone,” he warned. 

Maya swallowed hard. “Not alone?”

“Listen.” 

She heard the distant roar. Engines roared to life. 

“Move!” Null commanded. 

They sprinted ahead. 

Maya gasped as another siren joined in. “What’s that?” 

“Rogue units are activating. We’re out of time.” 

“Where are they?” 

Then she saw them. Figures emerged from the shadows. 

The silhouettes were ominous. 

“Null! They’re coming!” Maya shouted. 

“Stay close!” he shouted back. 

And then she realized. 

They were not only robots. 

They were the remains of the Iron Legion. 

“Null, they’re not—” 

But then a second body appeared.
