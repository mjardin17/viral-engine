Zeus looked over the barren hillside. “Without the thunderbolt, storms won’t come,” he said quietly. His fingers traced the empty space where the weapon usually rested.

Hercules sat beside him, frowning. “Without storms, there’s no rain.”

“And without rain?” Zeus continued. “Crops don’t grow. Rivers slow down. Greece will suffer.” 

His mind raced. “This isn’t just my problem. It’s everyone’s!” 

Hercules nodded, his tiny hands clenched. “All the flowers will droop. The people will stare at the sky.”

Athena joined them, her owl perched on her shoulder. “I’m glad you’re realizing this,” she said. “But why do you think Stormy took the thunderbolt?”

Zeus crossed his arms. “Stormy is just mean. He doesn’t want to share.”

Hercules added, “Maybe he thinks clouds are bad.”

Athena shook her head. “In two hundred years of observation, I’ve never seen Stormy act like this. Something has changed.”

“Changed how?” Zeus asked, puzzled.

“Something upset him.” Athena unfurled her scroll. “Things that upset others aren’t fixed by force. They get fixed by listening.”

Zeus felt a tightness in his chest. Listening? That idea felt foreign. He remembered his first week on Olympus. 

“I was lonely,” he said slowly. “Eating olives alone for three days? No one came to sit with me.”  

Hercules looked up. “But I did! I remember.”

“Yes, you did.” Zeus smiled at the memory. “You came without asking. You listened just by being there.”

Athena nodded. “That’s what Stormy needs. Someone to sit with him.”

“But he’s a cloud!” Hercules exclaimed. “How do we do that?”

Zeus stood up, determination growing. “We’ll find him. We’ll just… talk to him. Maybe we can understand what he feels.”

Hercules frowned. “But he might shock us! Lightning and all!”

“We have to try,” Zeus insisted. “This thunderbolt is bigger than me. It belongs to everyone.” 

“You think Storm is sad?” Hercules asked, his eyes wide.

“He could be. We don’t know yet,” Athena replied. “But if we don’t reach out, he’ll stay grumpy.”

Zeus pointed towards the horizon. “Let’s go. The longer we wait, the worse it gets.” 

They walked together, their small figures moving as one. The sun dipped low, casting shadows across their path. 

“What if he doesn’t want to listen?” Hercules asked, his voice shaky.

“He might be mad,” Athena answered, adjusting her glasses. “But anger is often hiding something deeper. Fear or sadness.”

Zeus felt an uneasy twist. He hoped she was right. 

“I’m worried,” Hercules mumbled. “What if Stormy doesn’t care about us? What if we can’t help?”

“Then we won’t give up,” Zeus declared. “We will listen anyway.” 

As they approached the gathering storm clouds, a loud rumble echoed across the sky. Zeus’s heart raced. Would Stormy accept them? 

Then, out of the black clouds, a massive shape loomed. It crackled with electricity.

“Stormy?” Zeus called out, voice steady. “Can we talk?”

The thunder rolled again, but suddenly, a dark form separated from the cloud. Zeus gasped, stepping back. 

A second body appeared, shrouded in darkness. 

“Who else is here?”
