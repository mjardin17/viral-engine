### Chapter 6: The Real Plan

Little Zeus floated ahead, flanked by his friends. They neared Stormy, who loomed large and menacing. Dark clouds billowed, flickering with uneasy lightning.

Little Zeus raised his hand. “We want to talk.”

Stormy paused. He expected a fight, not a plea. He flickered, torn between anger and curiosity.

“Who wants to talk?” Stormy growled, trying to sound fierce.

“I do,” Little Zeus replied. “Together, we want to understand.”

“Understand?” Stormy’s voice rumbled. “You really want to understand?”

“Yes,” Athena chimed in. “We’re here to listen.”

Stormy hesitated. He sensed sincerity, but it felt foreign. “Fine. Let’s get this over with.” 

Baby Hercules bounced with excitement. “Talk! Talk!” His enthusiasm was infectious, even to Stormy.

Little Zeus took a breath. “We’ve seen you. You make rain, and… you’re angry.”

“Angry?” Stormy thundered. “I’m not angry! I’m just... forgotten!”

His last word echoed, swallowed by the gathering clouds. Little Zeus tilted his head, encouraging him.

“Two hundred years,” Stormy continued. “Two hundred years of making rain nobody appreciates.”

The storm cloud expanded, crackling with frustration. “Farmers don’t wave anymore. Kids don’t come outside when I rain. They hide in their houses. They cheer for sunshine!”

Athena stepped closer, her owl ruffling its feathers. “What do you mean?”

“I mean,” Stormy shouted, “I don’t matter anymore! When did rain become a nuisance?” His voice softened, turning from thunder to a mere drizzle. “It hurts, you know?”

Little Zeus whispered, “We didn’t know.” 

Stormy blinked, confusion crossing his face. “Didn’t know what?”

“That you felt this way,” Baby Hercules said, flapping his tiny arms. “We thought…”

“You thought what?” Stormy sneered, yet his lightning dimmed.

Little Zeus shifted, gathering courage. “We thought you enjoyed the storms. We didn’t think anyone could be sad up here.”

Stormy hesitated. A silence enveloped them. 

“Being a storm cloud,” he finally said, “isn’t just about making rain. I miss people noticing me!” 

The three friends exchanged glances. They never knew how much Stormy longed for recognition. 

Athena spoke up. “What if we helped? What if we reminded people how important rain is?”

“Help?” Stormy’s clouds crackled softly. “What can you do?”

Little Zeus smiled. “We can create moments. Moments that matter.”

“Moments?” Stormy frowned. “How?”

The boy nodded earnestly. “We can work with you. Show everyone that rain brings joy, not just mess. We can remind them how to appreciate you!”

Stormy’s menace faded, replaced by intrigue. “You think it will work?”

“We believe it will,” Athena assured. “But only if you let us.”

Stormy’s lightning flickered uncertainly. “What if I’m not worthy of their attention?”

“Everyone matters, especially you,” Little Zeus replied. “You just need to let us show you.”

Stormy hesitated, conflicted. “Are you all really willing to help?”

“Absolutely!” Baby Hercules exclaimed, his arms outstretched like a bird ready to take flight.

Just as Stormy began to crack a smile, something streaked through the sky. A dark figure swooped past, casting shadows over them.

“What was that?” Stormy gasped, his uncertainty gusting back. 

With a low growl, the ominous figure faded into the distance. Lightning sparked once, then twice, with a warning.

“I don’t know,” Little Zeus said, eyes wide. “But we might be in trouble.”

Stormy’s clouds swirled again. “Trouble? What kind of trouble?”

Then they collectively glanced upward. A shadow loomed again — larger this time.

Then a second body appeared.
