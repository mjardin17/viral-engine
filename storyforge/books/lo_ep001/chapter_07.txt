### Chapter 7: Zeus Actually Listens

Zeus stuck his chin on his hands. He felt the soft, billowing cotton of Stormy beneath him. It was oddly comforting. He spoke slowly. “You mean you’ve been invisible all this time?”

“Exactly,” Stormy replied. He edged closer. “I’m huge! But no one sees me.”

Zeus frowned. “But you control the rain, don’t you?” 

“Sure,” Stormy grumbled. “But what does it matter if no one knows? They wave at me, and then stop. They barely notice.”

“What about the puddles?” Zeus asked. “You love making puddles, right?”

“Sure, they’re nice,” Stormy said, hesitating. “But I never see anyone jump in them!”

Zeus nodded thoughtfully. “That sounds exhausting. You’re not just a cloud. You’re Stormy.”

“I suppose.” Stormy’s voice fell to a whisper. “Every Tuesday, I drift alone. No significance.”

Zeus set his crown down. “I hear you, Stormy.” He paused, struck by the loneliness. “I’m sorry. That sounds really lonely.”

Stormy blinked, surprised. “You don’t have to feel bad for me.”

“No.” Zeus shook his head. “I have an idea. Not a kingly idea. A friend idea.”

Stormy perked up. “A friend idea?”

“What if, every Tuesday, it rained on Olympus?” Zeus asked, excitement bubbling. “Not just a drizzle. Proper rain! The kind that makes big puddles.” 

“You mean I would make rain for everyone?” Stormy leaned closer, hopeful.

“Yes!” Zeus clapped his hands. “And we’ll tell everyone it’s from you. That you’re important!”

Stormy lightened up a little. “Can the puddles be really big ones?”

“Absolutely yes!” Zeus exclaimed.

Hercules jumped up. “I’ll jump in every single one!”

Stormy allowed himself a small smile. “Okay. Let’s do it.”

The air buzzed with energy as Zeus grasped the thunderbolt. He focused on Stormy. “Okay, let’s make it rain!”

Stormy moved his dense body, and electricity crackled around him. “Here goes.”

With a sudden rush, Stormy opened wide. Rain poured out in torrents. It cascaded down onto Olympus. The droplets bounced off the marble, filling the streets. 

“This is fantastic!” Hercules yelled, darting outside. He plopped right into a puddle. The splash sent water flying high, catching Athena off guard.

She adjusted her glasses. “Hercules! Watch out!” 

“Sorry!” Hercules laughed, droplets drenching the area. 

Athena rolled her eyes. “You’re going to make a mess.”

Stormy let out more rain. The skies darkened, and soon it felt like a warm summer storm. The smell of wet earth saturated the air, bringing back life.

People emerged from their homes, delighted. Farmers cheered. Children shrieked with joy. Zeus grinned at Stormy, proud of his friend’s moment.

“This is it, Stormy! This is your day!”

But then, a low growl of thunder rumbled above. 

Stormy’s face clouded again. “What is that?”

Zeus stiffened. “I don’t know. The air feels strange.”

Just then, lightning streaked across the sky again, this time aiming for him.

“The killer had left a message: Read this, detective.”
