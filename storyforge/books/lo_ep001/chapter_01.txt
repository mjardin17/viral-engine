### Chapter 1: Welcome to Mount Olympus

Way up above the clouds, higher than birds and even taller than the tallest mountain, lay Mount Olympus.

On Mount Olympus, the gods lived. Most people thought the gods were serious and powerful. Most people had never met Little Zeus. 

Little Zeus stood at the edge of his bed. His wild curly white hair stuck up everywhere. He wore a tiny golden crown that always sat sideways. 

“Not again!” he sighed. 

Every morning, he followed the same routine. He jumped out of bed with a bounce and raced to the kitchen. 

“Want some breakfast olives?” he called to Baby Hercules, who was next door. 

“I love olives!” Baby Hercules shouted. He wobbled into the room, holding his tiny lion-skin cape. 

“Me too! I’m having twelve!” Little Zeus cheered. He munched on the olives as he walked back to his room. 

“Why are you always in such a hurry?” Baby Hercules asked, confused. He looked ready to break something. 

“Because I need to check my thunderbolt!” Little Zeus said. 

He dashed into his room, gulping down the last bite. His heart raced with excitement. The thunderbolt was his most prized possession. 

He reached for the golden stand. 

But it was empty. 

“Where is it?” he gasped. 

Little Zeus blinked. He rubbed his eyes. He glanced behind the stand and under it. 

“Not here!” he shouted. He searched inside his pillow. 

“Zeus! What are you yelling about?” Athena’s voice chimed in. She stepped into the room, her glasses slipping down her nose. 

“My thunderbolt is missing!” Little Zeus cried. 

“Missing? What do you mean missing?” Athena adjusted her glasses, sounding serious. 

“I mean gone. Poof! Vanished!” he exclaimed. 

Athena raised an eyebrow. “You always lose things, Zeus. Remember the toy lightning bolt?” 

“Not like this! I need it!” he shouted. 

“Calm down. We’ll find it,” she said, holding her scroll tightly. 

“Where would it go?” 

Suddenly, Stormy the Storm Cloud floated in, looking grumpier than usual. “What’s all the racket for?" 

“Stormy! Have you seen my thunderbolt?” Little Zeus asked. 

Stormy swirled with annoyance. “Why would I care about that sparkly thing?” 

“Because I can’t make lightning without it! No storms, no lightning!” 

Stormy frowned. “Less lightning means less trouble. I’m in.”

Before Little Zeus could react, Athena pointed out the open window. “Look! There’s a flash of light over the valley!” 

Little Zeus squinted. “Could it be my thunderbolt?” 

Stormy rolled his eyes. “Lightning doesn’t just fly away, kid.” 

“But it does!” Little Zeus shouted. 

Athena tapped her scroll, thinking. “Maybe it was stolen.” 

“Stolen?” Little Zeus felt panic rise. 

“Who would steal a thunderbolt?” Baby Hercules asked, scratching his head. 

Athena leaned closer. “You’d be surprised. Let’s go investigate.” 

But before they could move, Stormy scowled. “Hold on! I just saw something!" 

“What?” Little Zeus asked, fear creeping in. 

“Something big… and it’s trouble. We should hurry.” 

Then, out of nowhere, a second flash burst from the valley. 

“Then a second body appeared.”
