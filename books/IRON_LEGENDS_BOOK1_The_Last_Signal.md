## CANON (locked — matches IL_EP001)

- **Axon** — greatest guardian the Iron Legion ever produced. Thirty years dormant in a salvage yard outside Reno, Nevada. Rust doesn't stop what was built to last.
- **The Iron Legion** — ancient mechanical beings who arrived before humanity built its first city. Protectors. Builders. Warriors. They chose to stay hidden.
- **The Iron Council** — nine ancient mech gods governing from the Vault, buried beneath the Sahara. They decide who gets activated, who stays dormant, and who is too dangerous to ever wake.
- **Tagline:** *Roll Out. Transform. Remember Everything.*

## NEW FOR THE BOOK (original, no borrowed IP)

- **Mateo Reyes, 14** — works his uncle's salvage yard after school. Sharp with machines, bad with people. Lost his father three years ago; hasn't said the word "dad" out loud since.
- **Uncle Ray Reyes** — runs Reyes Salvage. Knows more than he says.
- **Vessel** — the Council's retrieval unit. Not a villain. A procedure with legs.

# ACT ONE — THE WAKING (Chapters 1–21)

## CHAPTER 1

The signal woke up at 3:47 in the morning.

Mateo Reyes did not.

He slept through the first pulse. He slept through the second. The third one came up through the floor of the trailer and rattled the water glass on his nightstand, and that woke him.

He sat up. He listened.

Nothing.

"Great," he said. "Now I'm hearing things."

He lay back down. He counted to ten. He got to four.

The fourth pulse hit hard enough to walk the glass off the nightstand. It shattered on the floor.

Mateo was out the door in eleven seconds, barefoot, in the cold.

The salvage yard stretched out under the moon — nine acres of dead cars stacked four high, corridors of rust running between them like streets in a city nobody lived in. Mateo knew every corridor. He'd mapped them when he was eight.

He knew that none of them had ever glowed before.

Blue light. Far end of the yard. Section Nine, where his uncle stacked the stuff too broken to sell and too heavy to move.

"Uncle Ray," Mateo said. Then louder. "Uncle Ray!"

No answer. Ray's truck was gone. Ray's truck was always gone.

Mateo went alone.

The gravel tore up his feet and he didn't slow down. The light got brighter as he ran — not flickering like fire, not sweeping like a searchlight. Steady. Patient. Like it had all the time in the world and had already used most of it.

He rounded the last stack and stopped.

There was a hand in the dirt.

Not a car part. Not a hunk of scrap that looked like a hand if you squinted. A hand. Four fingers and a thumb, each one longer than Mateo's whole arm, half-buried, palm up, like it had been reaching for something when it fell asleep.

The blue light was coming from between the knuckles.

Mateo had walked past that pile nine hundred times. He'd sat on it. He'd eaten lunch on it.

"No," he said. "No, no, no."

The fingers moved.

Dirt slid off them in sheets. Thirty years of Nevada dust let go all at once, and underneath it the metal was not rusted at all. It was silver. It was clean. It was warm — he could feel the heat from six feet away.

The hand turned over. It pressed flat against the ground.

And then, very slowly, it pushed.

The whole pile rose. Cars slid off it and crashed into the dirt like they weighed nothing, and the thing underneath kept standing up, and up, and up, until it blotted out the moon.

Mateo's neck hurt from looking.

Two eyes came on above him. Blue. The same blue.

They found him instantly.

A voice came out of the dark, and it was the oldest sound Mateo had ever heard.

"You are not the one I was waiting for."

## CHAPTER 2

Mateo did the smartest thing he had ever done in his life.

He said nothing.

"Answer," the voice said.

"I—" Mateo's mouth was dry. "I work here."

The blue eyes narrowed. Somewhere inside the chest above him, something enormous turned over, and the sound of it went through Mateo's ribs.

"Thirty years," the voice said. "Thirty years, and they send a child."

"Nobody sent me. I live here. In the trailer. With my uncle." Mateo pointed, then realized he was pointing at a stack of Buicks. "That way. Somewhere."

The giant looked where he pointed. It seemed to think.

"Your uncle," it said. "Describe him."

"Ray. Ray Reyes. He's — he's like fifty. He's got a beard. He wears the same jacket every day, it's got a burn hole in the—"

"Raymond."

The way it said the name stopped Mateo cold.

"You know him?"

The giant lowered itself. It didn't kneel — it folded, panels sliding across each other, joints reversing in ways that made Mateo's brain hurt to watch. In four seconds it went from forty feet tall to eye level with a fourteen-year-old boy.

Up close the face was almost human. Almost was worse.

"I know him," it said. "He buried me."

Mateo took a step back.

"He what?"

"He was younger than you when he did it. He promised to wake me when it was safe." The eyes dimmed a fraction. "He never came back."

"That's not — Ray wouldn't—" Mateo stopped. He thought about the truck that was always gone. He thought about Section Nine, the one part of the yard Ray never let him work. He thought about the way Ray got quiet every year in November.

"You're lying," Mateo said. It came out weak.

"I have many faults," the giant said. "That is not one of them."

Behind them, out past the fence, headlights swung onto the access road.

The giant's head turned so fast the air cracked.

"Get behind me," it said.

"What? Why—"

"Now, boy."

Mateo didn't move. "Who is that?"

The blue eyes went hard and cold and something in the giant's chest wound up like a spring.

"That," it said, "is why he buried me."

**[END CH 2 — THREAT cliffhanger]**

## CHAPTER 3

The headlights weren't headlights.

Mateo figured that out when they came through the fence without slowing down. Chain-link peeled off them like wet paper.

"Behind me," the giant said again.

This time Mateo moved.

He got maybe eight feet before a hand the size of a car door closed around him — gently, which was somehow the most terrifying part — and set him down in the gap between two crushed sedans.

"Stay."

"I'm not a dog!"

"You are considerably easier to replace than a dog. Stay."

The thing that came through the fence stopped forty yards out.

It was shaped like a man and moved like a clock. Every step landed in exactly the same rhythm as the one before. No wasted motion. No hurry. It carried nothing and needed nothing, and when it spoke, its voice was pleasant.

"Guardian designation Axon," it said. "Iron Legion. Third Cohort."

"Vessel," the giant said.

*Axon,* Mateo thought. *His name is Axon.*

"You are dormant," Vessel said.

"I was."

"That is a violation."

"It usually is."

Vessel tilted its head. "The Council did not authorize activation."

"I noticed."

"Then you understand my instruction."

"I understood it thirty years ago." Axon rolled his shoulders, and every panel on his body shifted a half-inch and locked. "I disagreed with it then."

Mateo's heart was going so hard he could feel it in his teeth.

"There is a human present," Vessel said.

"There is."

"That is also a violation."

"Add it to the list."

Vessel took one more step. Just one. The rhythm never broke.

"I am authorized to remove both violations," it said. "I would prefer you make that unnecessary."

And Axon — forty feet of ancient war machine, buried for three decades, woken up eleven minutes ago — laughed.

It was not a good sound.

"Vessel," he said. "How many of me have you retrieved?"

"Sixty-one."

"How many fought?"

A pause. Barely there.

"Fifty-nine."

"And how many won?"

Vessel said nothing.

"Say it."

"None."

"Correct." Axon's eyes flared. "Now ask me why I let him bury me."

Vessel's head tilted the other way. For the first time, its rhythm broke.

"Why," it said.

Axon leaned down until the two of them were inches apart.

"Because I was hiding from something," he said. "And it was not you."

**[END CH 3 — QUESTION cliffhanger]**

## CHAPTER 4

Mateo saw the whole fight.

It took four seconds.

Vessel moved first — a blur, straight line, no arc to it at all. Axon didn't dodge. He caught it. Both hands. The impact threw a wave of gravel over the sedans and Mateo covered his face.

When he looked again they were locked together, motionless, and the ground under them had cracked in a circle six feet wide.

"You are slower than the file said," Vessel observed.

"I'm older than the file said."

Axon twisted. Something in Vessel's shoulder gave with a sound like a bridge failing.

"Query," Vessel said, perfectly calm. "Why protect the human?"

"Wrong question."

"Correct the question."

Axon lifted Vessel off the ground, one-handed, by the throat.

"Ask why the Council sent a *retrieval* unit," he said, "for a guardian they marked *terminate*."

Vessel went still.

Truly still — not the frozen stillness of a machine holding position, but the stillness of a thing that has just been handed information it cannot process.

"That designation is not in my file," it said.

"I know." Axon's voice dropped. "Ask yourself who edited it."

He set Vessel down.

He let go.

Mateo, crouched in the gap between two dead cars, thought: *he's not going to kill it.*

Vessel stood there. Its shoulder hung wrong. It did not attack.

"I require verification," it said.

"Then go get it." Axon straightened to full height. "Ask the Ninth. Ask him directly. Not the Vault record — him. Watch his face when you do."

"The Ninth does not have a face."

"He does," Axon said. "That's the problem."

Vessel turned. It walked back toward the hole in the fence, in perfect rhythm, exactly as it had come. At the fence line it stopped.

"Guardian Axon."

"Vessel."

"If verification fails, I will return."

"I'd expect nothing else."

"If it succeeds," Vessel said, "I will also return."

And then it was gone.

Mateo waited a full minute before he came out. His legs didn't want to work right.

"Okay," he said. "Okay. Okay."

"You are repeating yourself."

"I'm allowed!" His voice cracked and he didn't care. "That thing came here to kill you and you just — you let it *leave*—"

"Yes."

"Why?"

Axon looked down at him. The blue eyes were dimmer now. Whatever had wound up inside his chest had wound back down, and he suddenly looked exactly like what he was: something very old that had just used more of itself than it had left.

"Because in about four hours," he said, "I am going to need him."

Then he sat down in the dirt, hard, and the light in his eyes went out.

**[END CH 4 — REVERSAL cliffhanger]**

## CHAPTER 5

Uncle Ray's truck pulled in at 5:20 a.m.

Mateo was sitting on Axon's knee. He hadn't moved in an hour and twenty minutes. The giant had not made a sound since the lights went out, but the metal was still warm, and Mateo had decided that warm meant alive.

The truck door slammed.

"Mateo!" Ray came around the stack at a run — and stopped so fast he nearly went down.

He looked at the forty-foot machine sitting in the middle of Section Nine.

He looked at his nephew sitting on its knee.

He put both hands on top of his head.

"Ah," he said. "Hell."

"You buried him," Mateo said.

Ray didn't answer.

"You buried him, and you told me Section Nine was unstable, and you told me the ground could give, and you made me *promise*—"

"Mateo."

"—you made me promise every summer since I was *eight*—"

"Mateo!"

"He said you were younger than me!" Mateo was standing now, shouting, and his voice went to pieces on the last word. "He said you promised to come back!"

Ray Reyes stood in his own salvage yard in the gray light before sunrise, and he aged about ten years.

"I did," he said quietly.

"Then why didn't you?"

"Because the second I woke him up, they'd know where he was." Ray's jaw worked. "And they'd come. And they'd bring the thing they always bring."

Mateo went cold.

"Vessel," he said.

Ray's head snapped up.

"What did you say?"

"It was here. An hour ago. It came through the fence."

Ray crossed the distance between them faster than Mateo had ever seen him move. He grabbed Mateo's shoulders.

"Is it still here? Is it in the yard?"

"No — Axon let it go, he said something about the Ninth, and it left, and then he just—"

"It left." Ray's face did something complicated. "It *left*."

"Yeah."

"Vessel doesn't leave." Ray let go and turned in a slow circle, hands back on his head. "Vessel has never once left. Sixty-one retrievals. Sixty-one. It does not leave."

"Well, it left."

"Then he's alive." Ray's voice broke on it. "God. Ray, you idiot. He's alive, he's actually—"

He stopped.

He was looking past Mateo, up at Axon's face.

The blue eyes had come back on.

"Hello, Raymond," Axon said. "You got old."

Ray Reyes, fifty-one years old, burn hole in his jacket, stood in the dirt and cried like a kid.

"You got rusty," he said.

"I did." Axon's voice was very gentle. "We have four hours. Possibly less. Sit down — both of you. There are things I should have told you when you were eleven."

**[END CH 5 — REVELATION cliffhanger]**

## CHAPTER 6

"The Iron Legion did not come here to protect you," Axon said.

The sun was coming up over the stacks. Mateo sat on an overturned bucket. Ray stood, because Ray never sat.

"We came here to hide," Axon said. "There is a difference, and the difference matters."

"Hide from what?" Mateo asked.

"That is the fourth question. We will get there." Axon's fingers spread flat on the dirt. "First: what do you know about the Council?"

"Nine of them," Ray said. "Under the Sahara. They decide who wakes up."

"That is what they say. It is even mostly true." Axon paused. "There were ten."

Ray's head came up. "What?"

"There were ten of us on the Council. Ten thrones in the Vault. Nine are occupied." Axon's eyes moved to Mateo. "Count the thrones sometime, if you ever see them. Everyone counts the gods. Nobody counts the chairs."

"What happened to the tenth?" Mateo asked.

"I am not certain."

"Guess."

"I do not guess."

"Then tell us what you *think*," Ray said. "You've had thirty years."

Axon was quiet for a long moment. Out past the fence, a semi went by on the highway, and the sound of it was so ordinary that Mateo almost laughed.

"I think," Axon said slowly, "the tenth is still sitting in that chair. I think he has been sitting in it for eleven thousand years. And I think that every activation order, every dormancy ruling, every retrieval Vessel has ever run — has come through him."

"Then he's the Ninth," Mateo said. "The one you told Vessel to go look at."

"No." Axon's eyes flared, hard and blue. "The Ninth is who he is pretending to be."

Ray sat down. Ray, who never sat down, sat down in the dirt.

"That's not possible," he said.

"It is deeply possible. It is the only thing that explains sixty-one retrievals with no survivors and no bodies." Axon leaned forward. "Raymond. Where do you think the sixty-one *went*?"

Nobody said anything.

And then, very faintly, from somewhere far under the ground beneath the salvage yard, something answered.

Four pulses.

The water glass in the trailer had already broken. This time it was the windows.

Axon stood up so fast the ground jumped.

"He heard me," he said.

**[END CH 6 — ESCALATION cliffhanger]**

## ACT TWO — THE SIXTY-ONE (Ch 22–48)
Recruiting the dormant. Each guardian = one moral cost. Mateo learns his father's death was not an accident. Vessel becomes an unwilling ally. Midpoint (Ch 35): the tenth throne is revealed empty — because he's been walking among them the whole time. Ray is taken.

## ACT THREE — THE VAULT (Ch 49–70)
Sahara. Descent. The sixty-one are found — repurposed, not destroyed. Axon must choose: free them (and lose the war) or leave them (and win it). He frees them. He loses. Ch 68: Axon falls.

## ACT FOUR — ROLL OUT (Ch 71–84)
Mateo, alone, finishes it. Not with a weapon — with the signal. He wakes every dormant guardian on Earth at once. The Ninth is unmade by the thing he spent eleven thousand years suppressing: all of them, awake, remembering.
Final line lands the tagline. **Series hook:** one guardian does not answer the signal. Book Two.
