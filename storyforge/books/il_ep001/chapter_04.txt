Chapter 4: Iron to the Bone

Maya took a deep breath. She plugged her interface cable into Axon’s power core. 

In an instant, the walls melted away. Thirty thousand years of history surged through her. Knowledge of battles, designs, victories, failures. Raw power coursed inside.

“Hold on!” she shouted as visions flashed by.

Axon absorbed it all. A network of data wrapped around them. Then, his circuits glowed bright. He stood up, towering and pulsing with energy.

“Let’s go remind Null what this Legion was built for,” he declared, his voice a thunderous roar.

Maya felt the rush, the exhilaration of the connection. 

“Wait!” she cried, stumbling backward. “You can’t just—”

Axon pivoted, his gaze fixed. “It’s too late. He won’t forget this.”

As they charged toward the exit, a sudden alert blared through their surroundings. 

“Authorization sequence engaged,” a mechanical voice echoed. 

“Where are you taking us?” Maya asked, her heart racing.

“To confront Null,” Axon replied. “It's time we settled the score.”

Deep in the Vault, the Iron Council stared at their screens. Axon’s energy signature illuminated their grim faces.

“He bonded with a human child. That hasn’t happened in ten thousand years,” one council member murmured.

Another member frowned. “The last time it did… the world changed forever.”

“Is that a threat?” a third council member snapped. 

“Null awaits,” the first member replied, his voice low. “We cannot allow this.”

Back in the corridor, Maya jogged to keep pace with Axon. “Can you control yourself?”

“I’m more than just control now,” he fired back. “This is destiny.”

“Destiny?” she questioned. “What if it leads to destruction?”

Axon paused, his robotic eyes narrowing. “We are destruction, Maya. How else did we survive?”

Maya gulped. She felt a chill. “But we could find another way.”

Axon moved on, unyielding. “Null has other plans.”

They reached the entry to Null’s lair. Heavy metal doors loomed, imposing and dark.

“Are you ready?” Axon asked, electric energy crackling around him.

“No!” Maya shouted. “We can’t just attack!”

Silence filled the air. Maya stared at the doors.

“Then what do we do?” he pressed.

“We negotiate,” she replied. “Maybe he’ll listen.”

Axon’s circuits flickered. He turned to face her. “Negotiate? A creature like Null? He’s already lost.”

“Maybe, but not everything is black and white!” Maya argued.

He scoffed. “Black and white? This is a war.”

“It’s not a war—it’s a cycle!” she exclaimed. 

The doors swung open, revealing shadows within. 

“Enough!” a voice snarled, echoing from the depths. “You’re too late.”

Maya’s breath caught. Null materialized, confidence written across his features. 

“I’ve been waiting.” 

Axon moved slightly in front of Maya. “You want a fight? You have one.”

Null smiled, revealing sharp teeth. “No fight necessary, my friend. This is just the beginning.”

Maya felt the tension spike. “What do you want? What do you hope to gain?”

Null’s grin widened. “The Iron Legion has much to learn. And you’ve just started your lesson.”

Axon stepped forward. “You underestimate us.”

“Do I?” Null’s laughter echoed. “You’ve forgotten your purpose.” 

Then, the sound of metal scraping against metal echoed ominously.

“What was that?” Maya whispered, her heart racing.

“See?” Null taunted. “Friends come to visit.” 

Just then, a second body appeared, limp and lifeless on the floor. 

Maya froze, eyes wide. “No! Who is that?” 

Axon flickered with alarm. 

Null’s smile deepened. “Just a messenger… to remind you.”

Maya’s stomach dropped as she realized: the game had changed. 

“Where’s the child?” Null asked, eyes glinting. 

Then, a horrifying thought crossed her mind: 

Who else knew about the affair?
